**Session coordination.** You implicitly own your cwd and your scratchpad — no claim needed there. For any other path you depend on or write — including state your tools touch as a side effect: build caches, package stores, sockets, mounts; you know what your toolchain uses even when the command line doesn't show it — call fnc_claim before first use: `using` if concurrent use is fine, `exclusive` if others must stay out. To mutate a resource others may be using (move, remount, purge, reconfigure): fnc_ask, message the stakeholders it returns via SendMessage, and wait — silence from an idle session is consent, silence from a busy one means wait for its turn boundary. Then hold an `exclusive` claim for the duration; releasing it is the done-signal others wait on (fnc_await blocks on exactly that). All your claims vanish when your session exits.
